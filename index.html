What it means for trading

Analytical views drawn from the data above, not trade recommendations.

Crude: a reserve refill is coming

The state reserve is well below where it started the year. A refill schedule from METI would become a steady bid for crude delivered to North Asia and for VLCCs into Japan. Watch for announced volumes, timing and grades.

Crude: some diversification will stick

Before the war almost all of Japan's crude came from the Gulf. US and Caspian barrels filled the gap. Expect more long-haul Atlantic Basin cargoes to Japan than before, even after Hormuz reopens.

Middle distillates: fewer exports into winter

Jet and gasoil stocks sit near the bottom of their range while exports recovered strongly in July and August. Either exports ease or Japan buys prompt cargoes, which supports Singapore gasoil cracks and the jet–gasoil spread.

Kerosene: winter upside risk

Kerosene stocks are below normal for August. A cold winter would pull imports from Korea and Singapore back up after this year's drop.

Naphtha: a buyer will return

Japan's naphtha imports fell sharply as crackers cut runs, and its import price swung to a discount to crude. When petrochemical margins allow restarts, a large buyer comes back to the Asian naphtha market.

Fuel oil: a structural surplus

Domestic fuel oil B/C demand has collapsed over the decade, so Japan keeps exporting residue into Asian fuel oil and bunker markets whatever happens to crude.

Designed for Middle East medium sour

Most Japanese refineries date from the 1960s–70s and were built to run Gulf crude. Residue desulfurizers and residue catalytic crackers need the heavy, sulphur-rich bottom of the barrel that light sweet crude does not provide.

Light sweet starves the upgrading units

Running more WTI raises naphtha and gasoline yields, cuts gasoil and jet, and leaves crackers short of feed. Refiners either blend with Gulf crude or buy vacuum gasoil and straight-run fuel oil to fill the units.

Some sites flex better than others

Taiyo Oil, with no residue desulfurizer, says it can run more than half its slate from outside the Middle East. Idemitsu cites equipment challenges. Rystad Energy puts the short-term ceiling for non-Gulf crude at roughly a third to a half of the slate, and Japan is already near the top of that range.

What it means for crude origination

Light sweet is close to its technical limit in Japan. The next long-term opportunity is medium-gravity crude, sour or low-sulphur, that fills residue units without Hormuz risk: Mexican and Ecuadorian grades, Brazilian pre-salt, or Gulf barrels loading outside the Strait.

US crude: what changes in the products, and what needs hedging

In August 2026 the United States supplied —% of Japan's crude, and the Middle East's share fell from —% in 2025 to —%. WTI is a different barrel from Arab Light: lighter, sweeter, priced off a different benchmark and shipped from the other side of the world. This page asks two questions: did Japan's refinery output change, and what new price risks do refiners now carry?

Why the product mix held

Government crude filled the gap

Japan drew about — million barrels from its stockpile. That oil is mostly Gulf crude, so refiners kept running a familiar sour slate while imports changed.

US barrels went where they fit

Sites without residue desulfurizers, such as Taiyo Oil, can take more light sweet crude. Others blend it with Gulf crude to keep their residue units fed.

The switch is recent

US volumes only became large from May, so a full product shift may not show yet. Watch PAJ's monthly production: a rising gasoline and naphtha share with falling fuel oil B/C would mean the slate is really changing.

What this means

So far the new exposure is mostly on the crude side (price basis, voyage time, freight, currency), not on the product side. If US crude stays, the product exposure follows.

Model yields are by weight and PAJ output is by volume, so compare the direction and size of the changes, not the levels.
